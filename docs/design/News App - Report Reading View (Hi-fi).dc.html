China's newest fab reshapes the global chip race

A state-backed plant pushes Beijing closer to advanced-node self-sufficiency — and adds fresh pressure on Taiwan and the US.

China's largest chipmaker began limited production at a new fabrication plant this week, a facility the government has positioned as central to its drive for semiconductor self-sufficiency. Early reports point to a 5-nanometre target — a node that would narrow the gap with industry leaders by roughly two generations.

Why it matters

Advanced chips underpin everything from AI training clusters to consumer phones. A domestic source at this node would blunt US export controls, which have curbed China's access to the most sophisticated tools since 2022.
